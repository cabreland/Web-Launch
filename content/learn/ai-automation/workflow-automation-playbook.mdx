Most founders automate the wrong thing first. They chase the flashy AI use case — a chatbot, a content generator — before fixing the boring plumbing that's actually costing them hours every week.

<OperatorNote>
When I automated intake-to-CRM handoff at my agency, we cut a 45-minute manual process to under two minutes. That's the pattern worth copying: automate the connective tissue between systems before you automate anything customer-facing.
</OperatorNote>

## The sequencing framework

1. **Map the manual handoffs.** Anywhere a human re-types or re-copies data between two systems is your first candidate.
2. **Automate the boring, high-frequency stuff first.** Low judgment, high repetition, low risk if something goes wrong.
3. **Only then touch customer-facing automation.** Chatbots, AI-drafted responses, and anything a customer sees directly should come after your internal plumbing is solid — otherwise you're layering AI on top of chaos.

## What actually breaks these builds

The failure mode isn't the AI — it's field mapping. Merge tag mismatches between your CRM and your automation platform are the single most common cause of "why didn't this fire" support tickets.
