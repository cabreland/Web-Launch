An earnout sounds simple on a term sheet: part of the purchase price now, the rest tied to future performance. In practice, it's one of the most contested mechanics in a deal — because the incentives of buyer and seller diverge the moment the ink dries.

<OperatorNote>
On deals I've been close to, the earnouts that worked had one thing in common: a metric simple enough to calculate from numbers both sides already agreed on — usually revenue, rarely EBITDA. The moment the earnout metric requires judgment calls, expect a dispute.
</OperatorNote>

## Where earnouts go wrong

- **Ambiguous metric definitions** — especially anything involving EBITDA adjustments post-close.
- **Loss of operational control** — the seller is accountable for a number they can no longer directly influence.
- **Buyer behavior that suppresses the earnout** — reallocating spend or sales in ways that happen to reduce the payout.

Structure the metric, the measurement period, and the seller's post-close authority before you agree to the number.
